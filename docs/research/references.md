## VI / blind keyboarding pedagogy

- **Perkins School for the Blind — *Starting from Scratch: Where Do I Start When Teaching My Visually Impaired Student to Type?***
  <https://www.perkins.org/resource/starting-scratch-where-do-i-start-when-teaching-my-visually-impaired-student-type/>
  Status: **saved locally** (HTML). Verified quotes: readiness = "keep hands in a 'resting position' … for a minimum of five to ten seconds"; **"Usually, that position is on the home row, but really any consistent position to start from is fine"**; warning that "starting from the beginning (home row, basic keyboard orientation) will frustrate your student to the point that he/she shuts down."
- **Teaching Visually Impaired — *Keyboarding Instruction for Students who are Blind or Visually Impaired***
  <https://www.teachingvisuallyimpaired.com/keyboarding-instruction.html>
  Status: fetched OK. Standard sequence "home row to other rows"; "cover keys to promote tactile memory."
- **National Federation of the Blind — *Typing: An Essential Skill* (Future Reflections 40:1)**
  <https://nfb.org/images/nfb/publications/fr/fr40/1/fr400103.htm>
  Status: **saved locally** (HTML). Verified quotes: "the typist always begins by placing the hands on the row of keys known as the home keys"; "Every keyboard I've encountered has built-in tactile indicators on the F and J keys … to help typists—sighted and blind—position their hands correctly."
- **Read&Spell — *Typing for the blind and visually impaired***
  <https://www.readandspell.com/us/typing-for-the-blind>
  Status: fetched OK. F/J raised lines as the orientation anchor; master one set of keys at a time.
- **See It Our Way — *Typing for Students with Severe Vision Loss***
  <https://seeitourway.org/typing-with-vision-loss/>
  Status: fetched OK. "Feel free to experiment with location, every student is unique" — flexible per-student anchor.

## Finger length, hand size & keyboard sizing

- **AIJFR (2025) — *A Mechanical Engineering Study of Finger Length, Typing Speed and Accuracy*** (`1578.pdf`)
  <https://www.aijfr.com/papers/2025/5/1578.pdf>
  Status: **saved locally** (PDF, 23 pp). Verified: studied **middle-finger length vs typing efficiency in Grade 6–12 students**; "moderate positive correlation: longer finger lengths were associated with faster typing speed (r = 0.43) and slightly greater accuracy (r = 0.366)." Caveats: self-measured finger length, online WPM test, survey method. Directly relevant — school-age population, middle finger specifically.
- **OhioLINK ETD (Warnaka) — *Relationship of Hand Size and Keyboard Size to Typing Performance Metrics*** (`ohiolink-hand-size-keyboard-typing-performance.pdf`)
  <https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=ohiou1385075311&disposition=inline>
  Status: **saved locally** (PDF, 79 pp). Verified scope: **touch-screen** keyboards (14/18/22 mm), 30 *adults* by hand size. Finding: typing speed/accuracy significantly related to **key size**; hand size less so → "a touch screen keyboard could be sized to the user's hand size." **Note:** touch-screen + adults — NOT a source for children's physical-keyboard hand dimensions (it was mis-cited for that earlier).
- **Porter, M. L. — *Getting Stuck: The Anthropometry of Young Children's Fingers***
  <https://files.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/17298684.pdf>
  Status: **paywalled — and no longer needed.** Was the intended source for children's hand-size figures, but the design moved to per-child calibration, which supersedes population statistics. Kept here for completeness only.

  <https://ergotype.pro/blog/09-column-staggered-keyboards/>
  Status: fetched OK. Middle finger highest, ring/pinky lower; columns offset by finger length (qualitative — no mm given on the page).

  <https://keebsforall.com/blogs/mechanical-keyboards-101/design-ergonomic-keyboard-layout>

## Motor learning, repetitions, and retention

- **Danna et al. — *Too little, too much: A limited range of practice 'doses' is best for retaining grapho-motor skill in children***
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475219302701>
  Status: abstract fetched (paywalled). Key finding: in 7–8 year olds, 90 repetitions (6 blocks × 15) → no long-term retention; 180 reps (12 blocks × 15) → robust gains and highest accuracy; 360 reps → similar gains but less accurate. Distributed practice (90 reps × 4 days) → outperforms massed 360 at 4–5 week follow-up. **Primary source for the Known minimum of 90 and rolling window of ~200.**

- **"Not for kids: 2nd grade school children require more practice than adults to attain long-term gains in a graphomotor task"**
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0885201422000946>
  Status: abstract fetched (paywalled). Adults but not children attained long-term benefits from low-dose practice. Reinforces children need more repetitions than adults for the same durable retention.

- **Acquisition of Motor and Cognitive Skills through Repetition in Typically Developing Children (PMC)**
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4934913/>
  Status: full text available. 90 typically developing children aged 6–12; three consecutive trials at a procedural task increased speed and efficiency. Supports the multi-session / spaced-practice model in the Takki age band.

- **Bönstrup et al. — *A Rapid Form of Offline Consolidation in Skill Learning* (Current Biology 2019)**
  <https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822(19)30219-2>
  PMC: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6482074/>
  Status: full text available. Motor skills consolidate during brief (~10 s) rest periods interleaved with practice, not just overnight. Supports the session-design principle that rest between attempts matters even within a session.

- **Cousins et al. — *Experience Playing a Musical Instrument and Overnight Sleep Enhance Performance on a Sequential Typing Task* (PMC)**
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4966959/>
  Status: full text available. Both sleep and musical experience led to greater improvement in typing performance in the 12 hours following learning. Direct evidence for sleep-dependent motor consolidation in a typing-adjacent task.

- **Danna et al. — *Practice schedule and testing per se affect children's transfer abilities in a grapho-motor task***
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022096521002411>
  Status: abstract fetched (paywalled). Follow-up to "Too little, too much." Compares single-session (24 blocks) vs. distributed (6 blocks × 4 days) in children. Key finding: by end of training and at 24h, massed practice leads in speed; by 4–5 weeks, distributed is equivalent or better and children show "delayed performance gains" (still improving) while massed shows deterioration. Distributed also outperforms on transfer tasks. **Needed: full paper for direct distributed vs. massed-12 (180 reps) comparison — not in abstract.**

- **Wikipedia — *Power law of practice***
  <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_law_of_practice>
  Status: fetched. Performance improves as log(repetitions); reaction time decreases linearly with log(trials). Justifies the rolling window being weighted toward recent performance (the oldest attempts are the lowest-value marginal evidence).

## Verification status of figures used in the notes

- **Verified from primary/local sources:** F/J as the universal tactile anchor (NFB); "any consistent position is fine," 5–10 s resting-position readiness, and the home-row-can-cause-shutdown warning (Perkins); middle-finger length ↔ typing speed/accuracy r=0.43/0.366 in school-age students (AIJFR); touch-screen key-size > hand-size for typing performance (OhioLINK).
- **Not needed (dropped from the argument):** children's hands ≈61/67/75% of adult size at ages 4/6/8. Intended source (Porter) is paywalled, but the design moved to per-child calibration, so population hand-size statistics are no longer an input to any decision. Not worth chasing.
- **Unverified but potentially citable (need a primary read first):** 19 mm pitch = 50–95th-percentile adult-male index finger; QWERTY ≈30% / optimized ≈74% home-row letter frequency; the ±5/±2.5 mm column offsets. Currently from search-engine summaries or blogs.
